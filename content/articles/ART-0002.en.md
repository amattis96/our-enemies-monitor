## A network crossing borders

In July 2024, four parcels dispatched from Vilnius entered DHL and DPD delivery networks. Three caused fires in Germany, Poland and the United Kingdom; the fourth was recovered intact. This is the sequence reconstructed by the European agency Eurojust in its 6 March 2026 statement [R1]. The incident raises a precise question: who organised the shipments, and for what purpose? The answer cannot be inferred solely from the authorities' suspicion of Russian military intelligence involvement.

## Chronology of events and investigations

**19 July 2024.** According to Lithuanian authorities, a Lithuanian citizen sent four parcels from Vilnius. Incendiary devices were hidden in everyday objects, including massage cushions and cosmetics. Two shipments were bound for the United Kingdom, two for Poland [R3, R4]. The contents and individual responsibilities form part of the investigative reconstruction.

**July 2024.** One parcel ignited at Leipzig airport before being loaded onto an aircraft, a second at a British warehouse and a third aboard a truck in Poland. The fourth remained intact, enabling technical examination [R1]. No explosion occurred aboard an aircraft. A potential threat to aviation must not be confused with an incident that actually occurred in flight.

**September 2025.** Lithuanian authorities announced an investigation involving fifteen people and attributed the organisation to individuals believed to have links to the GRU. LRT and Euronews reported the announcement [R3, R4]. This institutional attribution matters, but is not a final court judgment.

**21 October 2025.** Polish prosecutors announced a separate investigation into preparations for incendiary shipments bound for Ukraine, alleging that the suspects sought intimidation and destabilisation [R5]. Similar methods do not automatically prove a single command structure shared with the July 2024 events.

**7 January 2026.** Polish prosecutors filed an indictment against five people, four Ukrainian nationals and one Russian national, for alleged activity on behalf of Russian intelligence and sabotage using incendiary shipments. The indictment was announced on 16 January [R2]. An indictment is not a conviction.

**6 March 2026.** Eurojust stated that a joint investigation team from Germany, Lithuania, the Netherlands, Poland and the United Kingdom, assisted by Europol, had identified 22 suspects in Lithuania and Poland and brought two cases before courts [R1]. The 22 suspects, fifteen reported in 2025 and five defendants in the Polish proceedings have different scopes and must not be added together or treated as equivalent.

## The operational mechanism

Eurojust describes recruitment and instructions via online messaging, division of tasks and payments frequently made in cryptocurrency [R1]. Such an organisation can separate those ordering an operation from those preparing a device, transporting a parcel and dispatching it. This fragmentation may explain operational resilience, but does not prove that every participant knew the overall plan.

The agency also reports two test parcels sent to the United States and Canada and two more found in Amsterdam, likewise intended for North America [R1]. Reconnaissance of transatlantic routes is consistent with investigative concerns, but does not establish a definitive order to target a particular flight. The Guardian reported accounts from some suspects who claimed not to know the true nature of their assignments [R6]. These defences must be weighed against trial evidence rather than dismissed by assumption.

## Counterevidence, gaps and alternative interpretations

Institutional communications do not disclose all forensic material, original messages, financial flows or the complete chain of command. It is therefore impossible to verify independently who ordered each act. The presence of Ukrainian citizens among defendants does not attribute responsibility to the Ukrainian state; an alleged GRU connection does not justify attributing every European fire to the same organisation.

Operatives may have had different levels of knowledge. A courier may have known the purpose, or may have performed one stage without knowing the parcel's contents: individual evidence must distinguish these possibilities. The relationship between the 2024 shipments and preparations reported in October 2025 also requires independent documentary verification.

## European consequences and open questions

The vulnerability stems from the cross-border nature of logistics: a device introduced into one country's commercial network may set fire to an airport hub, warehouse or truck in another state. The joint investigation team is a concrete response to fragmented jurisdictions [R1]. Yet widespread compromise of courier firms or misconduct by their employees has not been demonstrated.

Three questions remain: what evidence connects operatives to alleged principals; what exactly do the overseas test parcels establish; and what findings will emerge from adversarial trials? These are verification leads for subsequent OSINT ingestion, not new edges in the graph.

## Conclusion

The fires and joint investigation document the risk of sabotage through commercial shipments. Authorities describe remote recruitment and allege a Russian intelligence role. Distinguishing proven harm, allegations and a chain of command not yet adjudicated is essential to a verifiable investigation.

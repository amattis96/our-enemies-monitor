## The question: can a European company lose its supply chain without losing its factories?

Nexperia is based in Nijmegen, the Netherlands, and makes semiconductor components used in the automotive industry, among others. Its ownership ultimately leads to China's Wingtech group through a corporate chain. In autumn 2025 a dispute over corporate governance, trade controls and access to production facilities became an international supply crisis. The question is not simply who owns the shares: **who can issue instructions, use intellectual property, package chips and deliver them to customers when a company operates across jurisdictions?** [R1][R2][R3]

The case provides a concrete example of European strategic dependence. It is not limited to advanced semiconductors: so-called *legacy* components are used in electrical and industrial systems and can halt an assembly line even when their unit value is low. Yet there is no proof that every disruption was designed by a government. Dutch decisions, court orders, Chinese restrictions, US rules and corporate conduct must be distinguished. [R1][R2][R4]

## A chronology of distinct powers

- **30 September 2025.** The Dutch economy minister invokes the *Wet beschikbaarheid goederen* (Goods Availability Act), an exceptional measure to protect production capacity. The government alleges improper transfers of assets, funds, technology and knowledge by management. This is an administrative rationale, not a criminal conviction. [R1]
- **4 October 2025.** According to a subsequent Dutch government letter, Chinese authorities impose an export control specifically affecting Nexperia operations in China. Licence requirements and delivery uncertainty affect customers. [R2]
- **October 2025.** The Enterprise Chamber of the Amsterdam Court of Appeal orders provisional measures, including suspension of a director, appointment of an independent director and temporary administration of almost all shares. This is a corporate proceeding independent of the ministerial order. [R1][R3]
- **6-19 November 2025.** Following talks involving China, the United States and the Netherlands, the Dutch government reports prospects for resumed shipments and suspends its ministerial order. That does not automatically revoke the judicial measures. [R1][R2]
- **11 February 2026.** The Enterprise Chamber orders an investigation into Nexperia's management and maintains temporary measures. It finds valid reasons to doubt proper management, while clarifying that a definitive finding of mismanagement belongs to a later stage. [R3]
- **2 September 2026.** The Gelderland court partly grants a request by shareholder holding company Yuching for preliminary witness examination, limits its scope and defers it until completion of the corporate investigation. The shareholder is thus also using procedural remedies, rather than merely being subject to Dutch decisions. [R5]
- **3-15 November 2026.** Nexperia brings a damages claim against Wingtech and its founder; Wingtech disputes the allegations and reports a claim of about $2 billion. On 11 November the Enterprise Chamber issues further measures; the following day the court-appointed officials announce they may explore alternative investors, without deciding to dilute Wingtech's ownership. Wingtech then announces its intention to appeal. These remain contested positions and proceedings. [R6][R7][R8][R9]

## The mechanism: four forms of control that do not coincide

**Economic ownership.** The Dutch court clarifies that temporary administration of shares is not expropriation: Yuching retains ownership but cannot normally exercise the majority of votes while the measures apply. Conflating shares and voting power creates a false narrative of permanent nationalisation. [R3]

**Corporate governance.** The court order suspends a director and assigns decision-making powers to independent officials. It is not the minister's order: the two authorities have different legal bases, purposes and oversight. The court found valid grounds for doubt and ordered an inquiry, not a criminal conviction of the director. [R1][R3]

**Physical production.** A semiconductor supply chain may separate wafer fabrication, final processing, testing, packaging and distribution. If Chinese sites do not supply finished components to other divisions, the availability of European factories does not automatically guarantee finished-product deliveries. The court-appointed officials say they are developing alternative capacity in Malaysia, the Philippines and with third parties; this is an announced strategy, not already proven replacement capacity in volume, timing and automotive qualification. [R2][R8]

**Commercial and financial access.** Chinese export restrictions and US rules affecting affiliates of controlled entities can influence licences, suppliers and credit. The Dutch government letter reports production stoppages at some customers, without quantifying total losses for European industry. A state restriction and a company decision remain distinct facts even when their effects coincide. [R1][R2]

## Who claims what

The **Dutch government** presents its intervention as protecting industrial continuity and security of supply; it stresses its separation from the corporate inquiry. That is its stated rationale (E2), not proof that every alleged transfer has been finally established. [R1]

The **Enterprise Chamber** describes conflicts of interest, unagreed strategic changes and the division of global operations as grounds for inquiry (E1, the content of a judicial act). Its February statement did not declare the investigation complete or definitively establish every responsibility. [R3]

The **court-appointed Nexperia officials** allege that actions by Wingtech and Chinese subsidiaries obstructed Dutch measures and that exploring new investors is necessary (E4 for the party's allegations; E1 for the existence of their statement). Their statement is not a neutral reproduction of the full judgment. [R8]

**Wingtech** rejects the damages claims, disputes the intervention and intends to challenge the latest ruling. Reuters reports these positions without treating them as a judicial finding in either party's favour. [R6][R9]

**Beijing** has challenged the intervention's legality and applied specific export controls; **Washington** separately exercises regulatory power over technology exports. Pressure from several states does not establish a single command structure among them or prove that every corporate decision was ordered by a government. [R1][R2][R4]

## The European impact: counting factories is not enough

Automotive manufacturing requires qualified components, documented reliability and continuous supply. Changing a packaging site or supplier requires testing, contracts and sometimes fresh qualification: nominal capacity is not capacity available to a particular customer. Dutch documents attest disruptions and stoppages at some firms, not a comprehensive accounting of European costs. [R2]

The case also exposes a legal vulnerability: intellectual property and corporate governance may be litigated simultaneously in different legal systems. An order by a Dutch court does not automatically make identical directions enforceable in China. Conflicting authorities and contracts increase uncertainty for customers and lenders. [R5][R8]

An alternative explanation matters: part of the crisis may result from overlapping trade controls and a governance dispute, without requiring an assumption of deliberate sabotage. Conversely, calling the matter a private dispute does not erase the effects of state export decisions. Both components must be measured separately. [R1][R2][R3]

## What remains unproven

The public material reviewed does not provide a full breakdown of lost volumes by site and customer, the relevant intercompany contracts and IP licences, the complete final reasoning of all parallel proceedings, independent verification of the time needed to replace Chinese capacity, or a breakdown of losses attributable to each decision. Operational coordination between the Chinese government and Wingtech to interrupt deliveries has not been established, nor has personal criminal wrongdoing. Damages allegations are not convictions. [R3][R6][R8]

Further scrutiny should compare the outcome of the corporate inquiry, any appeal decisions, changes in export licensing and disaggregated industrial data, without assigning personal responsibility that the records do not establish.

## Conclusion

Nexperia shows that European resilience cannot be inferred from a company's registered office or the nationality of its shareholder. Effective control over production stages, permissions, expertise and contractual relationships determines whether a component reaches the customer. **The supply disruption and judicial conflict are documented; a single geopolitical chain of command is not.** The investigation must preserve that distinction rather than fill it with speculation.
